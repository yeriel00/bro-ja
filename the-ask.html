The Ask

Simple by design

One function-focused question, shaped by our research with frontline clinicians, built to surface migraine in any kind of appointment.

“In the past month, has a headache interfered with your work, school, family, or other activities?”

Why this question

Fewer than 1 in 3 people with migraine reach diagnosis and recommended treatment.1 Our research found strong clinician support for universal screening, built on the postpartum depression model.2

  • Seconds to ask

    Because it takes only moments to ask, a single-question screen can become a routine part of every patient interaction.2

  • Function, not pain

    Patients often normalize symptoms or manage them quietly with OTC medication, so a pain scale can miss them. Function shows what’s at stake.2

  • Interfered, not missed

    One word widens the net. Patients who show up to work or school through symptoms would answer no to “missed” but yes to “interfered.”2

  • A topic worth raising

    Asking every patient signals that migraine is a legitimate clinical topic. For patients who stopped mentioning it, that can change what comes next.2

See the Data Behind the Movement

Built into the visit

Asking every patient matters more than who asks or when. The Ask is most effective when it’s built into the existing workflow, tied to a clear next step, and tested with your team before rollout.2

  • On the intake form

    Patients answer on a paper or digital intake form before the visit, so a “yes” arrives with the rest of their history.

  • During rooming

    A nurse or medical assistant asks alongside vitals and other routine questions, then flags a “yes” for the clinician.2

  • In the exam room

    The clinician asks while reviewing symptoms or the reason for the visit, and can follow up on a “yes” in the same conversation.

  • As an EHR prompt

    A prompt or template in the EHR reminds staff or the clinician to ask, so the question is asked even on the busiest days.2

When the answer is yes

A “yes” isn’t a diagnosis, but the start of evaluation. In primary care, recurring headache that disrupts daily life is most often migraine, and the American Headache Society offers guidance for every step, from workup and diagnosis to treatment and management.3

After the Yes: Guidance and Resources

Even with two minutes left

When a “yes” appears late in the visit, identifying the likely diagnosis and scheduling a follow-up still moves care forward.3

Share it with your team

“In the past month, has a headache interfered with your work, school, family, or other activities?”

The Ask works best when the entire care team knows about it, from the front desk handling intake forms to a nurse charting in the room.
