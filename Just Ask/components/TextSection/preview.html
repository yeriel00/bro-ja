If symptoms are not improving

Check access first. A treatment your patient couldn’t fill, afford, or take early can look like a failure, and quantity limits can lead patients to ration doses.1

Common reasons

  • Medication overuse
  • Taken too late
  • Dose too low or trial too short
  • Poor match between route and symptom
  • No antiemetic
  • Uncertain diagnosis

Review prescription and OTC medication use

Ask about OTC and combination products by name and how often your patient takes them. Many patients don’t count these as medication, and frequent use can lead to medication overuse headache.

When to involve a specialist

Migraine can often be effectively managed in frontline care. When specialist input is needed, you can continue providing care while coordinating a referral or consultation.1

  • Red flags that need urgent evaluation
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Prevention goals remain unmet after adjustment

Know when to consider prevention

Offer prevention based on how often symptoms occur and how much they disrupt daily life. We consider CGRP-targeting therapies a first-line option.1

Choosing a preventive treatment

Pick an option that also treats a comorbidity when possible, and weigh the side effects that matter most to your patient.

Give it time

Prevention succeeds when migraine days drop by half or more after a three- to six-month trial at the target dose.
