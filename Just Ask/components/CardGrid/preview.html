Why this question?

Many people with migraine do not receive a diagnosis and recommended treatment.1

  • Quick to ask

    A single question can fit into existing intake forms, rooming, or clinical visits. It takes just seconds to ask and can be documented alongside other routine screening questions, making migraine screening easier to incorporate into everyday care.

  • Interfered, not missed

    One word widens the net.

Identify red flags

  • Needs urgent evaluation

    • Fever
    • New neurological deficit
    • Thunderclap onset

  • Consider imaging or more tests

    • New onset after 50
    • Pain always on the same side
    • No response to treatment

What isn’t a red flag

Mid-face pain and pain at the base of the skull are common migraine symptoms.1
