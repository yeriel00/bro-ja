After the Yes · Step 3

Ongoing migraine care and management

A clear follow-up plan helps you assess treatment response, address access barriers, and adjust care. Schedule the next check-in before the patient leaves when possible.

  1. Step 1Diagnosing migraine
  2. Step 2Treating migraine
  3. Step 3Ongoing care

Plan the next check-in

  1. After a new acute treatment

    Once your patient has used medication three or four times.

  2. After starting prevention

    Reassess three to six months in, at the target dosage.2

  3. Once goals are met

    Every 6 to 12 months.2

Catch treatment failure early with a quick phone call, portal message or nurse check-in.

What to assess at follow-up

  • Number of migraine days and how much they disrupt daily life
  • How well and how fast acute treatment works
  • How often your patient uses acute and OTC medications
  • Whether your patient could fill, afford, and take treatment as planned

If symptoms are not improving

Check access first. A treatment your patient couldn’t fill, afford, or take early can look like a failure, and quantity limits can lead patients to ration doses.3

Common reasons

  • Medication overuse
  • Taken too late
  • Dose too low or trial too short
  • Poor match between route and symptom
  • No antiemetic
  • Uncertain diagnosis
Getting Treatment Covered

When treatment goals are met

Continue preventive treatment for one to two years, then discuss tapering or discontinuing. However, some patients choose to continue longer.4

  • Counseling Patients on Preventive TreatmentVideo

When to involve a specialist

Migraine can often be effectively managed in frontline care. When specialist input is needed, you can continue providing care while coordinating a referral or consultation.5

  • Red flags that need urgent evaluation
  • Pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Migraine-specific acute treatment still isn’t working after adjustment
  • Prevention goals remain unmet after adjustment

Consider an e-consult

A specialist can review the history and plan without a visit, so you continue care in the meantime.

What changes for specific patients

  • Hormones, contraception, and pregnancy

    Revisit the plan when your patient is planning pregnancy, is postpartum, or enters perimenopause, since migraine often changes during these stages.6

  • Children and teens

    Give families and schools a written action plan, and support school accommodations when migraine affects attendance.
