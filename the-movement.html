The case for asking

Migraine takes a heavy toll on work, family, and daily life, yet it often goes undiagnosed and untreated. One routine question can change that. Our research shows the path to care can be one conversation and one clinical tool away.

A common, disabling disease

Migraine is a complex, inherited neurological disease, with symptoms well beyond head pain.1 It spans every background and income and peaks in the working years, pulling people from work and family.2

The diagnosis gap

Most people with migraine see a clinician each year, often more than once, yet migraine rarely comes up. Many remain undiagnosed or misdiagnosed, and fewer still reach suitable treatment.

Wider for some patients

Diagnosis rates fall further for Black and Hispanic patients compared with white patients, and for men compared with women.8

What clinicians told us

Our research with clinicians across primary care, women’s health, urgent care, and specialty settings set out to understand how visits about migraine actually unfold. Surveys and interviews showed us where conversations stall, what helps them move forward, and what support would make a difference.9

Clinicians are ready

Clinicians told us they feel ready to manage migraine, and their confidence grows the more they diagnose it. What they asked for was practical support, like concise tools and clear next steps.9

Patients rarely raise it

Many patients describe their symptoms as “just a headache” or attribute them to stress, sinus trouble, or hormones, and over time they stop mentioning them. Waiting for patients to bring it up means many go undiagnosed.9

One question, asked of every patient

94%

of patients with episodic disabling headache in primary care had migraine.10

What’s often missing is a consistent moment to start the conversation. One function-focused question builds that moment into routine care, so you have the chance to ask, and no patient has to wait to be heard.
